**Problem 5.1 (Independence of mechanisms)** Let $P_X$ be the mixture of $k$ sharp Gaussian peaks at positions $s_1,\dots,s_k$ as shown in Figure 5.5, left. Let $Y$ be obtained from $X$ by adding some Gaussian noise $N$ with zero mean and a width $\sigma_N$ such that the separate peaks remain visible as in Figure 5.5, right.

1.  Argue intuitively why $P_{X\mid Y}$ also contains information about the positions $s_1,\dots,s_k$ of the peaks and thus $P_{X\mid Y}$ and $P_Y$ share this information.

2.  The transition between $P_X$ and $P_Y$ can be described by convolution (from $P_X$ to $P_Y$) and deconvolution (from $P_Y$ to $P_X$). If $P_{Y\mid X}$ is considered as the linear map converting the input $P_X$ to the output $P_Y$, then $P_{Y\mid X}$ coincides with the convolution map. Argue why $P_{X\mid Y}$ does *not* coincide with the deconvolution map (as one may think at first glance).

**Setup in formulas.** $P_X = \sum_{i=1}^k w_i\,\mathcal N(s_i,\sigma_0^2)$ with weights $w_i>0$, $\sum_i w_i = 1$, and $\sigma_0$ small; $Y = X + N$ with $N\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_N^2)$, $N \perp\!\!\!\perp X$, and $\sigma_0 \ll \sigma_N \ll \min_{i\ne j}|s_i-s_j|$.

**Definition (convolution).** For two probability measures $\mu,\nu$ on $\mathbb R$, their convolution is the probability measure

$$
(\mu*\nu)(B) := \int_{\mathbb R}\int_{\mathbb R} \mathbf 1_B(x+z)\,\mu(dx)\,\nu(dz), \qquad B \in \mathcal B(\mathbb R).
$$

It is the law of $X+Z$ when $X\sim\mu$, $Z\sim\nu$, and $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Z$ (by the product-measure/Fubini argument of Claim 1 in `anm_claims.pdf`). If $\mu,\nu$ have Lebesgue densities $p,q$, then $\mu*\nu$ has density

$$
(p*q)(y) = \int_{\mathbb R} p(x)\,q(y-x)\,dx .
$$

Convolution is commutative, associative and linear in each argument. In the problem, $P_Y = P_X * \mathcal N(0,\sigma_N^2)$, i.e. $p_Y(y) = \int p_X(x)\,\varphi_{\sigma_N}(y-x)\,dx$ with $\varphi_{\sigma}$ the $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ density; the map $p_X \mapsto p_X*\varphi_{\sigma_N}$ is linear and is exactly the kernel $P_{Y\mid X=x} = \mathcal N(x,\sigma_N^2)$ integrated against $P_X$.

**Definition (deconvolution).** Deconvolution is the inverse problem: given $q$ (the noise density) and the output $r = p*q$, recover the input $p$. Formally it is the inverse of the linear map $p \mapsto p*q$, whenever that inverse exists. With Fourier transforms $\widehat{p}(t) := \int e^{itx}p(x)\,dx$ one has $\widehat{p*q} = \widehat p\,\widehat q$, so

$$
p = \mathcal F^{-1}\big(\widehat r / \widehat q\big)
$$

provided $\widehat q$ has no zeros. For Gaussian $q$, $\widehat q(t) = e^{-\sigma_N^2 t^2/2}$ never vanishes, so the inverse exists on a suitable class of functions, but it divides by an exponentially small number at high frequencies: deconvolution is linear but ill-posed (small perturbations of $r$ blow up). Note it is a fixed linear map determined by $q$ alone; it does not depend on which $p$ produced $r$.

**Solution**

**a)** We have $X \xrightarrow{}[] Y, Y = X + N, N \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_N^2)$.

**Solution a)** If we choose $y$ midway between two adjacent peaks, say $s_1$ and $s_2$, then $P_{X\mid Y=y}$ puts its mass equally at $s_1$ and $s_2$ with a gap in the middle, so we know that we are not uniquely near any $s_i$. If we choose $y$ near $s_1$, then $P_{X\mid Y=y}$ has much more mass around $s_1$. Reason: $p(x\mid y) \propto p_X(x)\,\varphi_{\sigma_N}(y-x)$, and the factor $p_X(x)$ is $\approx 0$ away from the $s_i$, so for every $y$ the mass of $P_{X\mid Y=y}$ sits at the peak positions; $y$ only sets their relative weights $\propto w_i\,\varphi_{\sigma_N}(y-s_i)$. Hence $P_{X\mid Y}$ reveals $s_1,\dots,s_k$, just as $P_Y$ does, while $P_{Y\mid X=x} = \mathcal N(x,\sigma_N^2)$ does not.

**Solution b)** Fix the model $P_X=\sum_{k}w_k\,\mathcal N(s_k,\sigma_0^2)$, $Y=X+N$, $N\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_N^2)$, $N\perp\!\!\!\perp X$, and write $S:=\{s_1,\dots,s_k\}$, $s_{\min}:=\min S$, $s_{\max}:=\max S$,

$$
\kappa:=\frac{\sigma_0^2}{\sigma_0^2+\sigma_N^2},\qquad
v:=\frac{\sigma_0^2\sigma_N^2}{\sigma_0^2+\sigma_N^2},\qquad
m_j(y):=(1-\kappa)s_j+\kappa y .
$$

By Bayes (`deconv_helpers`), the posterior is $P_{X\mid Y=y}=\sum_j \pi_j(y)\,\mathcal N(m_j(y),v)$ with $\pi_j(y)\propto w_j\varphi_{\sqrt{\sigma_0^2+\sigma_N^2}}(y-s_j)$ (why?). The two maps on distributions:

$$
C(\tilde Q):=\text{deconvolution of }\tilde Q\text{ by }\mathcal N(0,\sigma_N^2),
\qquad
K(\tilde Q)(B):=\int P_{X\mid Y=y}(B)\,\tilde Q(dy).
$$

Both send $P_Y\mapsto P_X$ (why?). We show they agree *only* there, so $P_{X\mid Y}$ is not the deconvolution map.

*Representation.* A sample $X'\sim K(\tilde Q)$ can be generated in two stages: draw $\tilde Y\sim\tilde Q$; given $\tilde Y=y$, pick the index $J=j$ with probability $\pi_j(y)$ and set $X'=m_J(\tilde Y)+Z$, $Z\sim\mathcal N(0,v)$ independent (why?). Hence

$$
X' = (1-\kappa)S' + \kappa\tilde Y + Z, \qquad S':=s_J\in[s_{\min},s_{\max}] .
$$

All dependence on the intractable weights $\pi_j$ is confined to $S'$, of which we use only the bound $s_{\min}\le S'\le s_{\max}$.

*Sandwich.* If $X=A+W$ and $X''=A''+W$ on one probability space with $A\le A''$ a.s., then $P(X>x)\le P(X''>x)$ for all $x$ (why?). Applying this with $A=(1-\kappa)S'$ gives, for every input $\tilde Q$,

$$
\text{law of }(1-\kappa)s_{\min}+\kappa\tilde Y+Z
\;\preceq\; K(\tilde Q)\;\preceq\;
\text{law of }(1-\kappa)s_{\max}+\kappa\tilde Y+Z,
$$

where $\mu\preceq\nu$ means $\mu((x,\infty))\le\nu((x,\infty))$ for all $x$.

*Separating input.* Take $\tilde Q := P_Y(\cdot-t)
=\sum_j w_j\,\mathcal N(s_j+t,\ \sigma_0^2+\sigma_N^2)$ for a shift $t>0$ to be chosen. Writing $\tilde Y = S''+t+W$ with $S''\in[s_{\min},s_{\max}]$, $W\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_0^2+\sigma_N^2)$ (why?), the upper bound collapses:

$$
K(\tilde Q)\ \preceq\ \text{law of } s_{\max}+\kappa t+\kappa W+Z
= \mathcal N\!\big(s_{\max}+\kappa t,\ \sigma_0^2\big),
$$

using $\kappa^2(\sigma_0^2+\sigma_N^2)+v=\sigma_0^2$, which is the law of total variance $\operatorname{Var}X=\operatorname{Var}\mathbb E[X\mid Y]
+\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}[X\mid Y]$ in the single-peak toy (why?). On the other side, deconvolution commutes with translation (why?), so $C(\tilde Q)=P_X(\cdot-t)=\sum_j w_j\mathcal N(s_j+t,\sigma_0^2)$, and the same selection trick gives $C(\tilde Q)\succeq\mathcal N(s_{\min}+t,\sigma_0^2)$.

*Threshold.* The bounding Gaussians have equal width $\sigma_0$ and centre gap $(s_{\min}+t)-(s_{\max}+\kappa t)=(1-\kappa)t-\operatorname{diam}(S)$. Fix $z$ with $\Phi(-z)<\varepsilon$ and choose $t>\big(\operatorname{diam}(S)+2z\sigma_0\big)/(1-\kappa)$; let $x^\ast$ be the midpoint of the two centres. Then

$$
K(\tilde Q)\big((x^\ast,\infty)\big)\le\Phi(-z)<\varepsilon,
\qquad
C(\tilde Q)\big((x^\ast,\infty)\big)\ge 1-\Phi(-z)>1-\varepsilon,
$$

so $K(\tilde Q)\neq C(\tilde Q)$ and the maps differ. $\square$

*Moral.* $C$ is built from the noise alone and follows its input anywhere; $K$ carries the prior inside ($s_j,\kappa,v,\pi_j$) and every output is dragged back to within $\kappa$-reach of the old peaks. The two coincide exactly at the one input $P_Y$ that the old prior generated — Bayes’ inversion of *this* joint, not an inverse of the blur.
